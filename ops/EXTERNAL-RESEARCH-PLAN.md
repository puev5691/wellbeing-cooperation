# External cooperation research plan

## Purpose

Build a broad evidence-oriented corpus around cooperation, co-ownership, self-management, collective agency and economic democracy. The research should connect concepts to thinkers, practitioners, institutions, primary sources, criticism, failure modes and practical cases.

The corpus is not a canon of approved ideas. Similar words must not be treated as identical mechanisms.

## Main research lanes

- classical cooperative thought and early practice;
- mutual aid, mutualism, mutual societies and mutual banking;
- federalism, subsidiarity and polycentric governance;
- worker ownership, worker cooperatives and labor-managed firms;
- economic and industrial democracy;
- self-management / autogestion;
- commons and common-pool resource governance;
- social, collective and community ownership;
- solidarity economy, social economy and community wealth building;
- participatory economics and participation mechanisms;
- cooperative finance, credit unions and mutual insurance;
- cooperative federations and cooperative ecosystems;
- member capital, indivisible reserves and surplus distribution;
- community land trusts and collective infrastructure ownership;
- platform cooperativism and digital commons;
- collective agency, collective action and institution building;
- education for cooperative participation;
- successful and failed scaling cases.

## Seed names and cases

Search seeds include Robert Owen, the Rochdale Pioneers, Charles Fourier, Pierre-Joseph Proudhon, Peter Kropotkin, G. D. H. Cole, Karl Polanyi, Elinor Ostrom, Jose Maria Arizmendiarrieta and Mondragon, Jaroslav Vanek, Emilia-Romagna, Quebec social economy, ICA/CICOPA/ILO cooperative traditions, Yugoslav self-management, community wealth building and platform cooperativism.

These are discovery seeds only, not a closed list and not an assertion that all belong to one school.

## Evidence rule

Preferred source order:

1. original works, primary documents, bylaws, reports and datasets;
2. official cooperative/federation/institutional sources;
3. academic books and papers;
4. high-quality historical and critical reviews;
5. journalism and popular material mainly as navigation to stronger sources.

For every strong claim record source role, locator, evidence strength, limits, criticism and conflicts.

## GitHub-ready outputs

Expected research layer:

- external source registry;
- subject registry for thinkers/practitioners/organizations/cases;
- claim queue;
- concept map;
- people-and-practice map;
- case map;
- criticism/failure map;
- cards for people, organizations and cases;
- primary/secondary source indexes;
- public manifest.

Full third-party texts require a separate publication-rights basis. Locator, metadata and original project analysis can be published without silently implying republication rights.

## Relation to the Bobrovsky / Chartaev corpus

The external search runs in parallel with the Bobrovsky and Chartaev source work. Similarities are comparison candidates until both sides are independently sourced and reviewed.
